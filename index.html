Learning Is Knowing the Unknown Through the Known

“Learning is knowing the unknown through the known.” — Shaik Imran

Meaning

Learning begins with what we already know. When we encounter something unknown, we use existing knowledge, experience, observation, and reasoning as a foundation for understanding what we do not yet know.

In this sense, learning can be viewed as a movement from the known toward the unknown, with the known serving as a bridge to new understanding.

The idea behind the quote

I formulated this sentence from my own reflection on learning. The quote expresses my concise formulation of the idea that we use what is already understood as a means of approaching what is not yet understood.

This page records the wording and attribution of this formulation. It does not claim that the broader philosophical idea of moving from the known toward the unknown is historically new.
